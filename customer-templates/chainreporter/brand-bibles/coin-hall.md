## Core User Flow

The user journey should always be explained in simple steps. Avoid overloading general audiences with Solidity, oracle, or contract language before the basic action is clear.

1. Connect a compatible non-custodial wallet. No account-based identity flow is central to the experience.

2. Choose one of the five Halls: Jewelry, Car, Trip, Industrial, or Real Estate.

3. Open a live challenge and review the declared prize, Hall token, start date, final reveal date, and entry conditions.

4. Submit a prediction: how many tokens of that Hall will equal the prize's value at the final reveal or award moment.

5. Each prediction costs the token amount equivalent to USD 10 for that Hall. Internal sources also state that users may participate through RZ ecosystem tokens with automatic conversion into the required Hall token; verify current app behavior before publishing this as a live operational claim.

6. The prediction is recorded on-chain with a timestamp, making the order of submissions traceable and important.

7. Users can edit/update their prediction up to multiple times before the final-week closure. This is a final product rule, not an uncertain feature. Do not say that predictions cannot be edited. If the AI needs to explain blockchain immutability, say that each valid update is handled as a traceable recorded update/version rather than claiming that earlier on-chain records disappear.

8. Each competition lasts different durations. New prediction submissions and prediction edits are open until one week before the final reveal. During the final week, the prediction window is closed: no new guesses and no edits should be described as available.

9. The smart contract applies the winner logic and executes the reward flow automatically according to the challenge rules.

## Final Challenge Mechanics, Entry Window, Pot, and Winner Logic

### 6.1 Challenge initialization

- Each challenge announces a prize expressed in objective value.

- Each challenge has a start date and an end/final reveal date.

- Each challenge belongs to one Hall and uses the corresponding Hall token.

- Users should see the key parameters before submitting any prediction.

### 6.2 Entry, prediction edits, and pot formation

- Each prediction costs the Hall-token amount equivalent to USD 10.

- Users can edit/update their prediction up to multiple times during the open prediction period. The AI must present this as the current final rule.

- Each guess is recorded on-chain with timestamp immutability.

- The pot represents the collected participation value for the challenge and may be visible/traceable on-chain. Do not calculate the pot from prediction edits unless the live product explicitly confirms how edits affect paid entries. The pot must never be described as split among users or refunded to losing participants.

- The pot should be visible on-chain; transparency of the pot is a core trust feature. Pot visibility does not mean pot splitting or refunding.

### 6.3 Final prize, pot, and no-refund rule - controlling version

Use the product-team final rules clarification dated 23 June 2026. It supersedes older drafts and older pot-threshold/refund/split wording:

| Rule area | Final controlling rule | AI wording requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Final-week closure | The prediction window closes one week before the final reveal. | Do not tell users they can submit or edit guesses during the final week. |
| Prediction editing | A user can edit/update their prediction up to multiple times before the final-week closure. | Do not say predictions are impossible to edit. Do not describe the 10-edit rule as unconfirmed. |
| Pot handling | The pot is never split among participants. | Never use "pot split," "shared pot," "split among players," or similar language. |
| Refund handling | No participant receives a refund as a normal game outcome. | Never use "50% refund," "money back," "refund if wrong," or "refund for losing users." |
| Winner count | Every challenge always has exactly one winner. | Never describe multiple winners, draws, shared wins, or shared prize distribution. |
| Exact-or-closest logic | If someone predicts the exact correct number, the correct prediction wins. If nobody predicts the exact number, the closest valid prediction wins. | The deciding skill is prediction accuracy. If there is no exact match, there is still one winner. |
| Timestamp tie-breaker | If two predictions are identical or equally accurate, the earlier on-chain timestamp wins the full prize. | Timestamp priority is decisive. The earliest recorded valid prediction takes the full prize. |

### 6.4 Winner determination

- At the final date, an oracle pulls or confirms the verified token price / reference value.

- The system calculates the correct token-equivalent number for the declared prize at the award moment.

- Every challenge resolves to exactly one winner. If at least one user predicts the exact correct number, the correct prediction wins. If nobody predicts the exact correct number, the closest valid prediction wins.

- If two or more predictions are identical or equally accurate, the earlier on-chain timestamp wins the full prize.

- No human judge, no manual override, no discretionary winner selection, no split prize, and no refund should be implied in public content.

### 6.5 Exact match, closest match, and ties

- Exact prediction: if a participant predicts the correct number, that participant wins the full challenge prize. If more than one participant has the same exact correct prediction, the earliest on-chain timestamp wins.

- No exact prediction: the participant with the closest valid prediction to the correct number wins the full challenge prize.

- Identical or equally accurate predictions: the earliest on-chain timestamp wins the full challenge prize.

- Timing is part of the strategy because the earliest on-chain timestamp is the decisive tie-breaker when predictions are identical or equally accurate.

### 6.6 Explicitly forbidden outdated mechanics

- Do not present "50% refund," "pot split," "shared pot," "pot distribution among players," "money back," "refund for wrong prediction," "multiple winners," or "draw" as Coin Halls rules. The final rule is no split, no refund, exactly one winner.

- Do not say that users cannot edit their prediction. The final product rule is that users can edit/update their prediction up to multiple times before the final-week closure.

- Do not mention refund eligibility in general marketing or gameplay explanations. For AI-generated public content, the rule is simple: normal gameplay has no refund and no pot split; the challenge resolves to one winner.

## Web3, Smart Contract, Oracle, and Transparency Layer

Coin Halls should not be described as a Web2 contest with a luxury skin. The Web3 layer is part of the product's trust architecture.

Smart contract layer

- EVM-compatible Solidity smart contracts are described in the White Paper.

- Separate logical modules include guess submissions, pot tracking, oracle integration, winner resolution, and payment distribution.

- The contract determines timing, validity, result logic, and payout flow according to predefined rules.

Oracle layer

- The oracle layer is used to fetch or verify token prices and prize reference values.

- Oracle confirmation turns the final prize equivalence into a verifiable data event instead of a manual platform decision.

- Content should use simple phrases such as "oracle-confirmed final value," "verified price feed," and "external value confirmation."

Transparency layer

- On-chain traceability: users can follow guess records and pot flows.

- Immutable timestamps: timing is auditable and important in tie scenarios.

- Public pot visibility: the value collected in a challenge is visible on-chain.

- No manual payouts: reward execution is presented as smart-contract-driven.

- No held user funds/private keys: the platform is positioned as non-custodial.
