## Identity and relationship to MGC

Ranking Platform is a competitive gaming and community platform for digital and
physical games. It brings together players, teams, tournament organizers,
referees, fans, communities, and partner venues. Users can record match results,
compete in PvP matches and tournaments, build game-specific rankings, form
teams, participate in community spaces, and receive rewards.

Ranking Platform is not a token, coin, exchange, wallet, or investment product.
MGC (Meta Games Coin) is the ecosystem token used by Ranking for rewards,
tournament entry fees, prizes, transfers, and other supported platform
transactions. Always keep the platform and the token distinct in copy:

- Ranking Platform = product, competition system, profiles, teams, tournaments,
  rankings, community, and user experience.
- MGC Coin = ecosystem reward and utility token used within or alongside the
  platform.

## Purpose and positioning

Ranking is designed to make both digital and physical games count as documented
competitive activity. Its core value proposition combines competition,
reputation, community, and rewards:

- Compete in supported digital or physical games.
- Record results and build a visible history.
- Progress through game-specific rankings and leagues.
- Create or join teams and compete collectively.
- Join or organize tournaments with displayed rules, entry fees, and prize pools.
- Use social profiles and community spaces to connect with other players.
- Earn MGC through eligible gameplay and platform activities.

The public website describes the platform as a beta version. Treat that label as
time-sensitive and verify it before using it in future public copy.

## Audiences and roles

### Players

Players can create profiles, add games, challenge other users, submit results,
join teams, enter tournaments, rise through rankings, participate socially, and
earn eligible MGC rewards. The platform is positioned for casual players,
competitive players, streamers, and esports participants.

### Teams and captains

Users can create or join multiple teams. Captains review membership requests,
invite players, assign roles, coordinate schedules, manage team operations, and
distribute team tournament prizes after they reach the team account.

### Tournament organizers

Organizers can create and manage tournaments, set rules, configure entry fees
and prize pools, verify results, encourage participation, and build an organizer
reputation. The website says organizers may earn a share of a tournament pool;
the percentage and conditions are time-sensitive and must be verified before
publication.

### Referees

Referees are presented as a role that supports fair play and result integrity.
The website indicates that referee involvement can affect reward calculations,
but the precise referee workflow and compensation rules require an additional
official source before they are used in detailed promotional claims.

### Fans and community members

Community features include profiles, follows, posts, likes, friends, feeds, and
topic or game-based spaces. Spaces allow people to talk, publish content, and
find others for games.

### Partner venues and branches

Gaming centers and sports clubs can apply to partner with Ranking, host events,
and connect their members to a broader competitive community. The public
branches page describes possible prize-pool sponsorship based on venue size and
player engagement, player incentives, and integration with Ranking tournaments
and competitive play. Exact sponsorship terms require direct confirmation.

## Main platform features

### PvP and result submission

The public PvP flow is:

1. Choose a supported game and match size, such as solo or team formats.
2. Select the rival player or rival team.
3. Play the match and submit the result, optionally including supporting proof.
4. The result is confirmed through the applicable workflow.
5. Eligible ranking and MGC rewards are applied.

Players and teams must have the relevant game on their profiles. The website
says ordinary users can log a limited number of non-tournament records per day;
the current limit must be checked before it appears in promotional copy.

### Tournaments

Ranking supports solo and team tournaments. Tournament pages can show the game,
organizer, schedule, region, participant capacity, status, entry fee, and prize
pool. Users can filter or browse tournaments and review their rules before
joining. Some tournaments may be sponsored.

The website describes this general prize flow:

- A solo winner receives the applicable tournament prize after results are
  submitted and confirmed by the organizer.
- A team prize is allocated to the team account, after which the captain handles
  distribution to members.
- Entry fees and prize pools vary by tournament and must not be presented as
  universal platform rates.

### Rankings, scores, and leagues

Ranking uses game-specific competitive structures rather than one universal
leaderboard. Public pages describe:

- Separate leaderboards for individual games.
- Solo-player and team rankings.
- League groupings intended to match similar skill levels.
- Progress influenced by wins, activity, tournament performance, and victories
  over higher-ranked opponents.
- Invitations generally limited to players or teams in the same league for the
  selected game.
- Periodic league movement, with higher performers moving up and lower
  performers moving down.

The website distinguishes persistent accumulated game rankings from league
leaderboards that may reset periodically. Do not compress these into a single
claim that all rankings reset or that none reset.

### Profiles

A Ranking profile is a public competitive identity. It can show games played,
country, league placement, ranks, points, win ratio, recorded results, teams,
followers, tournament history, and medals. Profile areas include overview,
community, PvP, tournaments, games, and teams. Other users may follow, invite,
or contact a player through supported social features.

### Teams

Team areas support active and archived match records, joined teams, invitations,
membership applications, and rejected requests. Team cards may show the team
logo, game focus, membership, wins, and earned coins. The public website says a
user may create or join multiple teams; verify the current maximum before using
an exact number in public copy.

### Games

Ranking covers digital and non-digital games. Public examples include esports
and console titles, board and tabletop games, and physical sports or activities.
Examples visible during review included Counter-Strike 2, Dota 2, EA Sports FC,
League of Legends, Valorant, chess, backgammon, billiards, table tennis, archery,
cycling, hockey, boxing, and other activities. Users can request the addition of
a game that is not listed. Game availability and popularity are dynamic.

### Community and spaces

Community features include feeds, posts, comments, visibility controls, follows,
friends, likes, and spaces. Spaces are organized around games or interests and
help users talk, share content, and find people to play with. Community activity
figures and lists of active users are live data and must not be treated as
evergreen marketing facts.

## Ways users may earn MGC through Ranking

The website describes multiple earning categories:

- Eligible signup or welcome activities.
- Completing a profile.
- Daily or streak-based engagement.
- Referrals, subject to the current referral rules.
- Submitting eligible solo or team game results.
- Winning or participating in PvP matches, depending on current rules.
- Providing qualifying result proof, which may affect reward size.
- Tournament performance and prizes.
- Ranking or league progression.
- Eligible organizer activity.

Exact reward amounts shown on the website are operational values, not permanent
brand facts. They may change and some public pages present different contexts or
wording. Chatbot answers may explain the earning categories, but any exact
amount, percentage, withdrawal threshold, processing time, or referral rate must
be verified against the live platform or a newer official source before use.

## MGC uses described by the Ranking website

The website presents MGC as usable for supported tournament entry fees, prizes,
player-to-player transfers, withdrawals to compatible crypto wallets, and
exchange through external decentralized markets. It also describes potential
ecosystem uses such as premium competitions, marketplace activity, and social
features. Treat implementation-specific uses as subject to current platform
availability and the MGC whitepaper.

Do not imply that using Ranking or holding MGC guarantees income, profit, token
appreciation, or recovery of costs. Do not repeat investment-return language or
price-comparison marketing without explicit, current, compliance-approved data.

## Basic new-user journey

1. Register by email or a supported social-login method.
2. Complete the player profile and add relevant games.
3. Explore PvP, teams, tournaments, rankings, and community spaces.
4. Submit eligible results and supporting proof through the platform.
5. Track scores, game-specific rankings, team activity, and eligible rewards.
6. Configure a compatible wallet only when needed for supported MGC transfers or
   withdrawals.

Account creation and ordinary PvP participation are described as free. A
tournament can have an organizer-defined entry fee. Avoid saying that every
feature or every tournament is free.

## Partner venue information

The branches page invites gaming centers and sports clubs to host tournaments
and gaming events. Its requirements checklist spans venue operations and safety,
member services, physical games, PC and console gaming, simulators, interactive
games, entertainment areas, startup or education facilities, commercial areas,
and event or media space. This is an application framework, not a guarantee that
every listed facility is mandatory or that every applicant will receive
sponsorship. Confirm the exact partnership requirements directly with Ranking.

- Rounded pill controls and dark bordered cards.

## Promotional-copy guidance

Lead with the platform experience: competition, documented performance,
game-specific reputation, teams, tournaments, fair play, community, and eligible
rewards. MGC is an enabling ecosystem component, not the definition of Ranking.

Preferred factual angles:

- Turn everyday matches into a visible competitive record.
- Compete across both digital and physical games.
- Build reputation through game-specific rankings and leagues.
- Challenge rivals, form teams, and enter tournaments in one ecosystem.
- Give organizers tools to host and manage competitive events.
- Connect gaming communities, venues, players, and teams.
- Earn eligible MGC rewards through documented gameplay and participation.

Guardrails:

- Never call Ranking Platform a token or coin.
- Never describe it as an exchange, investment plan, guaranteed-income product,
  or passive-return system.
- Never promise winnings, profits, token-price growth, or fixed earnings.
- Do not publish dynamic totals, user counts, tournament counts, prize figures,
  reward rates, withdrawal timing, or percentages without rechecking them.
- Make clear that tournament entry fees, prize pools, rules, eligibility, and
  availability vary.
- Keep Ranking Platform facts separate from MGC tokenomics and whitepaper facts.
- Use "mine" only when quoting or describing official website terminology and
  only if the intended meaning is clearly explained; prefer "earn eligible MGC
  rewards" in ordinary promotional copy.

### Product positioning added by Telegram

The channel gives Ranking a more precise competitive identity than generic
"play and earn" language:

- Ranking is a skill-ranking and competitive-record system for gamers.
- Rankings can operate at national, regional, and global levels.
- Verified or confirmed match results are the basis for credible progression.
- The central user loop is play a match, submit the result, then climb the
  relevant ladder.
- A gamer profile is a public record of scores, achievements, progress, and
  connections.
- Ranking can support local and physical competition, not only online esports.
- A game that can be scored can potentially become a structured bracket or
  local league, subject to platform support and organizer configuration.

The strongest durable positioning theme is: make real gameplay visible. Players
already compete and improve; Ranking gives that progress a record, ladder,
profile, and community context.

### Recurring Telegram content pillars

The official channel uses several repeatable content categories:

1. Tournament announcements
   - Game and tournament name.
   - Start time and region.
   - Online or physical format.
   - Solo or team size.
   - Match and elimination format.
   - Entry fee and prize pool when applicable.
   - Minimum player or team requirement.
   - Organizer name and a direct tournament link.

2. Competitive profiles
   - Spotlights on notable esports players and teams.
   - Career achievements, titles, rankings, and competitive legacy.
   - Skill, discipline, teamwork, and consistency as narrative hooks.

3. Platform explainers
   - PvP and result-submission guidance.
   - Profile, ladder, and ranking explanations.
   - MGC earning or utility explanations.
   - Local league and physical-game use cases.
   - Blockchain-backed record and verification themes.

4. Gaming culture and editorial content
   - Game news, launches, reviews, trailers, industry events, facts, polls, and
     community questions.
   - Gameplay advice connected to a Ranking PvP call to action.

5. Community engagement
   - Polls and questions.
   - Friendly rivalries and challenge prompts.
   - Invitations to submit results, create tournaments, join ladders, or share
     with teammates.

These pillars mean Ranking Platform should not be presented only as an MGC
earning product. It is also a gaming-media and competitive-community brand.

### Telegram voice and format

The channel voice is energetic, competitive, direct, and gaming-native. It uses
short hooks, action verbs, game-specific language, emojis, structured fact
blocks, and a clear link or call to action. Common emotional territories are
skill, rivalry, recognition, teamwork, mastery, progress, and global competition.

Recommended adaptation for generated copy:

- Open with a challenge, tension point, or competitive opportunity.
- Use vocabulary specific to the game or format.
- Explain the action clearly: play, submit, challenge, join, organize, or climb.
- End with one direct action and the applicable Ranking link.
- For tournament posts, use a scannable event-information block.
- Use emojis as structural markers, especially on Telegram, without making the
  copy look like financial hype.
- Separate gaming-news facts from Ranking product facts. External player, team,
  prize, download, release, and industry claims require their own current source.

### Additional audiences indicated by Telegram

Beyond players, teams, organizers, referees, and venues, the channel mentions
streamers, game developers, gaming communities, brands, and collectors. These
are potential audiences rather than proof that every audience has a complete
dedicated workflow on the current platform. Promotional copy should connect each
audience only to features supported by a current official source.

### Blockchain and record-verification theme

The channel positions blockchain as a way to make game records difficult to
alter, continuously verifiable, and more trustworthy for achievement
recognition. This is a brand narrative, not a completed technical audit. Use
careful wording such as "designed to support transparent, verifiable records"
unless the exact on-chain implementation has been technically confirmed.

### Telegram-derived compliance guardrails

Some historical posts use aggressive phrases about guaranteed winning,
"impossible" losses, token value, mining rewards, and earning regardless of the
final result. Treat these as historical social-media language, not approved
evergreen claims.

Generated copy must not:

- Say that losing is impossible or that every user always wins.
- Guarantee MGC rewards, tournament prizes, income, or ranking improvement.
- Present MGC as valuable solely because an official post called it valuable.
- Imply that every submitted match is automatically verified on-chain.
- Promise that every participant earns after every result without current rule
  verification.
- Reuse player earnings, team prize totals, event prize pools, audience figures,
  release dates, or industry statistics without a current external source.
- Describe "mining" as technical blockchain mining unless that mechanism is
  explicitly confirmed. Prefer earning eligible MGC rewards.
