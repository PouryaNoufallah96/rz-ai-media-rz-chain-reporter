The word “Bank” is part of the brand name and product metaphor; it is not evidence that CoinBank is a licensed traditional bank or deposit-taking institution.

## Purpose and customer problem

Web3 users often move between multiple wallets, chains, bridges, token platforms, dashboards, and payment systems. Access may be technically possible while control, permissions, fees, routes, maturity rules, liquidity, and risk remain difficult to understand.

CoinBank’s product story is ownership-first digital finance: organize selected on-chain actions in one clearer interface while keeping final authorization with the user. The value proposition is visible rules, wallet-based consent, structured execution, cross-chain access, and practical utility—not speculation or guaranteed wealth.

Explain the user journey before the architecture:

**Connect wallet → choose a supported action → review route and terms → sign or approve → smart-contract logic executes → track status and records**

Always identify what the user controls, what permission is being requested, what the smart contract does, and what risks or conditions remain.

## Primary audiences

Write for:

- Self-custody users and digital-asset holders.
- People managing assets across supported blockchain environments.
- Users seeking structured early access or rule-based allocation.
- People interested in practical digital-asset payment and card utility.
- Web3 builders, fintech teams, and institutional readers interested in transparent execution, compliance structure, and verifiable rules.
- Readers evaluating RWA, AI-finance, credit, protection, or institutional modules as future product directions.

Assume financial awareness without assuming deep protocol expertise. Explain unfamiliar terms clearly and avoid presenting eligibility or suitability as universal.

## Wallet control and architecture

Use this separation consistently:

- The interface organizes available routes, products, terms, and status.
- The user-controlled wallet authorizes transactions and permissions.
- Smart contracts enforce the defined logic for supported actions.
- Networks and bridges carry execution according to their own conditions and risks.
- Foundation-led structures provide oversight, upgrade, reporting, and governance direction where documented.

The current platform’s core principle is: **If the user does not sign, the action does not happen.** This does not mean every signature is safe. Wallet prompts may authorize token allowances, contract permissions, routes, fees, or continuing access. Good CoinBank content helps users understand what will change, which asset and amount are involved, who receives permission, whether permission remains active, and what happens if execution fails.

Never say smart contracts eliminate vulnerabilities, independent review guarantees safety, or foundation oversight gives the platform custody. Blockchain transactions may be irreversible, and lost wallet access may not be recoverable by CoinBank.

## Current public product pillars

### Capital Movement

The current official platform describes cross-chain capital movement executed from the user wallet rather than inside a custodial platform. It displays USDT routes associated with Ethereum, Tron, and BNB environments and references RZUSD within the flow.

Describe this as wallet-controlled cross-chain access or capital movement. Do not promise that every displayed route, token, network, fee, limit, speed, or liquidity source is currently available. Never say cross-chain execution is instant, free, riskless, or reversible.

### Structured Early Access

CoinBank presents structured early access to token offerings under defined rules. The documented flow is: the user purchases through a wallet, tokens are locked by smart contract, the allocation is visible in the dashboard, and release follows the defined schedule.

Use `structured early access`, `pre-sale access under defined rules`, and `dashboard-visible allocation`. Do not promise preferred pricing, appreciation, liquidity, listing, profit, or an exit unless the specific current offering terms prove it.

### Capital Allocation

The current platform describes a rule-based allocation process in which the user chooses an eligible token, amount, and duration; signs from their wallet; and interacts with smart-contract-defined maturity and participation logic.

Describe the mechanism and its disclosed conditions, not the outcome. The official page currently displays incomplete or zero-valued participation figures, while older materials contain other performance language. Therefore never quote an allocation rate, monthly benefit, calculated return, duration, maturity payment, APY, or profit figure as evergreen content. A numerical claim requires a current, directly cited product term and human review.

### CoinBank Token

CoinBank Token is presented as a fixed-supply asset designed for access, allocation, and participation inside the CoinBank financial layer rather than as an isolated speculative token. Current official material describes a BEP-20 context with future expansion, foundation-based governance, controlled release, and burn mechanics.

Use **CoinBank Token** or **COINBANK**. Do not use `RZBank` unless quoting and clearly identifying an outdated source conflict. Supply information is contradictory: current pages may display zero or incomplete values, legacy tokenomics says 100 million, and the whitepaper/external material refers to 1 billion. Never publish a supply, circulating amount, price, market cap, holder count, contract address, exchange availability, burn amount, or ranking without current contract or approved market-data verification.

Allocation percentages and burn language may describe the documented design, but they must not be used to imply scarcity-driven price appreciation. Token utility must be explained through access, participation, allocation, fees, rewards, or ecosystem interaction only when the relevant feature is supported by a current source.

### CoinBank Debit Card

The official website presents a debit-card proposition intended to connect eligible on-chain assets with real-world payments through conversion at checkout. The documented user flow is to connect a wallet, link eligible assets, and request or use card access.

Use **CoinBank Debit Card** and describe it as `designed to connect on-chain assets to real-world payments`. Do not promise that a card has been issued, that every applicant is eligible, that conversion is instant, that all assets are supported, that there are no separate compliance requirements, or that it works everywhere major cards are accepted. Current card availability, issuer, network, fees, geography, KYC, limits, and settlement behavior require direct verification.

### RZ ecosystem connections

Current official material connects CoinBank with Coin Mining for token distribution, RZChain for execution and settlement, RZ Prime for structured early access, RZ Foundation for oversight and structure, Ranking for market visibility, and Meta Coin Guard for optional protection.

Keep product roles distinct. CoinBank is the financial and capital-access layer; SLT CargoPay is cargo settlement; RZ Prime is structured early access; Meta Coin Guard is protection or cover; Ranking is market visibility; and RZChain is infrastructure. Do not merge these brands or attribute one product’s capability to another.
