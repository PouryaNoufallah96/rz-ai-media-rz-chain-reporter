SLT CargoPay is non-custodial Web3 payment and settlement infrastructure designed for cargo transportation and logistics workflows. It connects cargo invoices, Transportation IDs, payment links, wallet confirmation, smart-contract coordination, payment stages, settlement history, and traceable on-chain transaction records.

The strongest short definition is:

> SLT CargoPay helps users create cargo invoices, generate Transportation IDs, share payment links, confirm transactions through their own wallets, and record settlement on-chain using supported assets.

## Positioning and category boundaries

Always position SLT CargoPay as cargo transportation payment infrastructure, a logistics settlement dApp, or a smart-contract settlement layer for operational cargo workflows. Its reason to exist is to make payment coordination clearer across real transportation processes.

SLT CargoPay is not a general consumer checkout, generic crypto-payment app, bank, exchange, broker, custodian, insurer, trading platform, market maker, lending service, factoring service, escrow provider, securities product, or guaranteed-return product. It does not manage seed phrases, hold user private keys, or sign wallet transactions for users.

Do not use the conflicting website-footer phrase **“parametric cover system.”** The supplied knowledge brief identifies it as inconsistent with the primary product definition and explicitly supersedes it unless a later approved source confirms otherwise.

The supplied documents do not establish an approved legal entity name, registration jurisdiction, licence, regulatory status, audit, insurance arrangement, reserve attestation, or universal geographic availability. Never invent or imply any of these.

## Purpose and customer problem

Cargo transportation often involves several parties, documents, payment moments, due dates, confirmations, and manual follow-ups. Screenshots, messages, and disconnected records can make it difficult to determine what was requested, which stage was paid, which wallet confirmed, and which transaction belongs to which transportation process.

SLT CargoPay addresses that coordination problem by connecting operational payment context with wallet-controlled confirmation and on-chain transaction evidence. The value proposition is clarity, traceability, structured stages, and a shared payment record—not speculation or token-price promotion.

Lead with the operational problem before explaining the blockchain mechanism. A useful content sequence is:

1. Identify the cargo, invoice, coordination, or settlement problem.
2. Explain how the SLT CargoPay workflow structures it.
3. Show what the wallet, smart contract, and transaction hash each prove.
4. State the limitation: payment evidence is not proof of a physical cargo event.
5. End with a practical implication or question for logistics operators.

## Product architecture and source of truth

Use this role separation consistently:

- The dApp displays and organizes the payment workflow.
- The user-controlled wallet reviews, signs, and confirms the transaction.
- Smart contracts coordinate the defined settlement logic.
- The transaction hash provides the on-chain payment record.
- The dashboard and Settlement History organize workflow status and transaction evidence.

Never imply that SLT CargoPay can sign on behalf of a user, recover a private key, reverse an irreversible wallet transaction, or unilaterally override smart-contract outcomes.

On-chain payment evidence proves the payment state recorded by the relevant transaction. It does **not** by itself prove that cargo was loaded, inspected, released, delivered, or accepted. Physical milestones require appropriate off-chain documents, systems, or trusted confirmations. Likewise, a completed payment workflow reflects payment status, not automatic completion of the underlying cargo journey.

## Core workflow

The canonical high-level workflow is:

**Create Invoice → Generate Transportation ID → Share Payment Link → Wallet Confirmation → On-Chain Settlement**

Keep these concepts distinct:

- **Invoice:** the payment request and its commercial context.
- **Transportation ID:** the operational identifier connecting the cargo-payment workflow and its stages.
- **Payment link:** the shareable route through which the payer reviews the request.
- **Wallet confirmation:** the user-controlled approval and signing step.
- **Settlement:** execution and recording of the defined on-chain payment action.
- **Transaction hash:** the verifiable blockchain record associated with that action.

Do not shorten the workflow in a way that suggests SLT CargoPay controls the user wallet or independently certifies cargo delivery.

## Product modules

### Quick Invoice

Quick Invoice is the single-step cargo-payment workflow. The user creates an invoice, selects a supported asset, generates the relevant Transportation ID and payment link, shares the request, and tracks the wallet-confirmed settlement record.

Use Quick Invoice for content about direct cargo payment requests, simpler payment coordination, payment links, wallet approval, and transaction-hash records.

### Cargo Transportation Process

Cargo Transportation Process is the public name for the staged or multi-step transportation-payment workflow. `Multi-Payments Invoice` may be recognized as an internal or technical alias, but public explanations should prefer **Cargo Transportation Process** or **multi-step payment**.

This workflow organizes multiple payment stages, terms, due dates, and separate confirmations under transportation context. Do not invent stage names, Incoterms, release conditions, counterparties, or due dates for a specific transaction.

### Conditional payment

The supplied material describes conditional payment options including time-based locking and optional third-party confirmation. Explain them as configurable settlement conditions. Never call them legal escrow, guaranteed delivery protection, insurance, or proof that a physical milestone occurred.

### Settlement History and operational records

Settlement History, invoice status, operational notes, and confirmations help users follow the payment workflow and connect records to cargo operations. Describe them as operational and transaction records, not as a substitute for accounting, customs, legal, compliance, or cargo-management systems.

### Treasury Program

The Treasury Program is part of the approved SLT CargoPay content scope. Describe it structurally as smart-contract-coordinated participation for supported assets, with wallet-based interaction and dashboard-visible records according to current platform rules.

Do not present Treasury as a bank account, savings product, deposit guarantee, passive-income opportunity, insured investment, security, risk-free yield, or promise of returns. The supplied sources contain conflicting presentations of rates and durations. Therefore evergreen content must not quote an exact rate, monthly percentage, total return, duration, or profit calculation. Any time-sensitive numerical Treasury statement requires a current, directly cited approved source and human review before publication.
